# Privacy Policy — Picture Cross (חושף התמונה)

**Effective date:** September 6, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how Picture Cross ("the App," "we," "us"), a picture-logic puzzle game for iOS also known by its Hebrew name חושף התמונה, handles information when you play. We wrote this policy to be a plain, accurate description of what actually happens in the App — not a generic template — so please read it if you have any concerns about your privacy.

## 1. What the App Is

Picture Cross is a single-player Nonogram (picture-logic) puzzle game. You fill in cells on a numbered grid to reveal hidden pixel-art pictures. The App has no user accounts, no login, no sign-up, no chat, no user-generated content, and no multiplayer features of any kind.

## 2. Information We Do Not Collect

We do not operate any backend server for the App, and there is no developer-controlled server that stores or receives your personal information. Specifically, we do not collect, and have no means of collecting:

- Your name, email address, phone number, or any account credentials (there is no account to create)
- Your precise or approximate location
- Your contacts, photos, camera, or microphone data
- Health, fitness, or biometric data
- Payment or financial information (the App currently has no in-app purchases — see Section 7)

## 3. Information Stored on Your Device Only

To let you keep your progress, the App saves the following data locally on your device, using Apple's standard `UserDefaults` storage:

- Which levels you have completed
- Your play streak
- Achievements you have unlocked
- Booster counts and other in-game currency
- Your preferences (for example, light/dark mode)

This information never leaves your device. We do not transmit it to us or to any third party, because no such transmission path exists in the App. If you delete the App from your device, this data is permanently deleted along with it. We have no way to recover it, back it up, or access it remotely, because we never had a copy of it in the first place.

## 4. Third-Party Services (Advertising)

Picture Cross is free to play and is supported by advertising. We use **Google AdMob** to serve ads, including:

- Interstitial ads shown after completing certain levels
- Rewarded ads that you may optionally choose to watch in exchange for an extra life, a booster, or another in-game bonus
- An optional banner ad on the level-select / gallery screen

AdMob is operated by Google, and when ads are requested and displayed, Google (and its advertising partners) may collect and process information such as your device's advertising identifier (IDFA), general device information, and interaction data, in order to serve and measure ads and, where permitted, personalize them. This data collection and processing is governed by Google's own privacy practices, not by us. We do not have access to, and do not separately collect or store, the data Google's SDK gathers.

You can review Google's practices here: https://policies.google.com/privacy

You can also review Google's specific information for AdMob and how ad personalization works through Google's Ads Settings and the About Google Ads page.

## 5. App Tracking Transparency (ATT) and Your Choices

Because AdMob's advertising identifier can be used to track you across apps and websites operated by other companies, iOS requires us to ask for your permission before that tracking occurs. The first time it is relevant, you will see Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt asking whether Picture Cross may track your activity across other companies' apps and websites.

- If you **allow** tracking, you may see ads that are more personalized to your interests, based on data collected by AdMob and its partners.
- If you **decline** tracking (or if tracking is otherwise unavailable, for example because Limit Ad Tracking is enabled system-wide), the App still works exactly the same and you will still see ads — they will simply be non-personalized, based on limited contextual information rather than your cross-app activity.

You can change your choice at any time in **Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking** on your device, where you can see and manage tracking permissions for every app, including Picture Cross.

## 6. European/UK Users — Consent for Personalized Ads (GDPR)

If you are located in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, or Switzerland, the App uses Google's **User Messaging Platform (UMP)** consent flow. Before any request for a personalized ad is made in these regions, the App presents a consent form (built and served through Google's UMP SDK) asking whether you agree to personalized advertising and the associated data processing. If you do not consent, or before you have made a choice, only non-personalized ads are requested. You can typically revisit and change your consent choice from within the App's settings, where a "Privacy Options" or equivalent control is provided by the UMP flow.

## 7. In-App Purchases

Picture Cross does not currently offer any in-app purchases, and no payment or purchase functionality is active in the App. We are considering a future optional "Remove Ads" purchase, but as of this policy's effective date it has **not been implemented or released**. This Privacy Policy will be updated before any such purchase becomes available, describing exactly what payment-related information (handled entirely by Apple's App Store, not by us) would be involved.

## 8. Children's Privacy

Picture Cross is a general-audience puzzle game suitable for all ages, but it is **not designed, marketed, or directed specifically at children under 13**, and it is not listed in the App Store's "Kids Category." We do not knowingly collect any personal information from children. Because the App itself collects no personal information on any user, this is true for users of every age. Ad personalization is also subject to Google AdMob's own age-based and consent-based screening, and where applicable laws (such as COPPA or GDPR-K) require it, ad requests are automatically restricted to non-personalized, child-appropriate treatment by Google's systems. If you believe a child has provided us with personal information, please contact us using the details in Section 12 — though as explained above, we do not have the technical means to receive such information through the App in the first place.

## 9. Data Retention

Because the only data associated with your play of the App lives on your own device, retention is entirely in your control:

- Gameplay data (progress, streaks, achievements, boosters, settings) is retained locally for as long as the App remains installed.
- Uninstalling the App deletes this data permanently.
- We retain nothing on any server, because we have no server that stores it.

Data collected by Google AdMob (such as advertising identifiers and ad-interaction data) is retained and governed according to Google's own retention practices, described in Google's Privacy Policy.

## 10. Your Rights (GDPR, CCPA, and Similar Laws)

Depending on where you live, you may have rights such as the right to access, correct, delete, or port your personal data, or to object to or restrict its processing. We want to be transparent about how these rights apply here: since Picture Cross does not collect or hold any personal data about you on any server we control, there is generally no developer-held personal data for us to provide, correct, delete, or export on your behalf. In practice:

- **Access / deletion of local data:** You can view your own gameplay data simply by using the App, and you can delete all of it at any time by uninstalling the App — no request to us is required or able to reach data we never had.
- **Advertising data held by Google:** Requests relating to data collected by AdMob (for example, IDFA-based advertising data) should be directed to Google, using the controls in Section 5 (ATT settings) and Section 6 (UMP privacy options), or directly via Google's own privacy tools and policy.
- **Questions or concerns:** You are always welcome to contact us at the email address in Section 12, and we will do our best to help, even where a technical "data request" is not applicable because no personal data exists on our end.
